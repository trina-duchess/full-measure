Nutrients, What They Do, and Where to Get Them

How to log

Use + Add a food at the top of the food list to add anything new from its Nutrition Facts label. Foods you add show an Edit button so you can fix or delete them.

Drag a food from the list into a meal box to add one serving. Drop it again to add another, or use − and +. Tap × to remove it. On a phone or tablet, press and hold a food for a moment, then drag.

Water counts the water in everything you log: food, coffee, tea and Diet Pepsi. The daily target is 91 oz of total water from food and drinks (National Academies). Caffeinated drinks count toward it.

Carbohydrate (130 g minimum) is what your brain needs for fuel. Omega-3 ALA (1.1 g) is the plant omega-3 your body can't make; walnuts, ground flax, chia and canola oil are rich sources. Saturated fat is a limit: under 10% of calories.

The tracker puts the nutrient you're furthest from meeting at the top. Limits (sodium, added sugar, caffeine) are listed separately, since less is better there.

Where the targets come from

Daily targets are the National Academies' Dietary Reference Intakes for women 51–70 (RDA, or AI where no RDA exists), as published in the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets. Sodium and added-sugar limits come from the Dietary Guidelines for Americans; the caffeine limit is FDA guidance. Food values come from USDA FoodData Central and your own product labels. Iodine values are approximate, and the pasta is an estimate.

Your weight

Sets your protein goal for building muscle: 1.4 g per kg of body weight, the middle of the 1.2–1.6 g/kg range that strength-training studies support. Until you enter a weight, the goal is 90 g. Saved on this device.

Sync across devices

Paste a GitHub token with the gist permission. Your log and added foods will be kept in a private Gist and shared by every device you connect. The token is saved on this device only.

USDA food lookup key

Paste the key USDA emailed you. It's saved on this device only, never in the app's code. Without it, lookups use USDA's shared demo key (a few per hour).
